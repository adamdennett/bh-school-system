A strategic view

Brighton & Hove’s secondary school system

In ten years this city has to run a school system for roughly 760 fewer children a year than it has now. This site is an attempt to say — from published data, in the open — what that actually means, and what the options are.

Brighton & Hove has ten secondary schools, a cohort that peaked in 2019 and is falling, and a sequence of admissions decisions each argued about on its own. What has been missing is an account of how the whole system behaves: where the children are, how families choose, what each policy does to every other school, and which combinations leave the city with a viable set of schools in 2035.

That account is below, together with a simulator you can put your own scenarios through. Everything here is built from published data. No pupil records are used anywhere.

On the data. Everything on this site is built from published sources: DfE performance tables and the statistics API, the council’s own allocation factsheets going back to 2010, the 2021 Census, OpenStreetMap and the real bus timetable, and one Freedom of Information request. The code is open on GitHub. No pupil-level data is used anywhere.
